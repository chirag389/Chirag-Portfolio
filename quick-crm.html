02 · Context

Creating a centralized customer system

Quick CRM is the centralized customer management system within the Quick Hub ecosystem. When I started working on this product, there wasn't a proper way to manage customers. Contact information was scattered across Quick Chat, Quick Campaign, and Quick Ads, with each product storing only basic details like name, email, and created date.

There was no shared customer profile, activity history, or reusable customer data. My focus was to build a centralized CRM that brought customer information into one place and later evolve it with segmentation to support campaigns and broadcasts.

There were contacts everywhere, but there was no centralized customer system.

03 · The Problem

The Problem

We had customer records across Quick Hub, but businesses didn't have a connected way to manage and use them.

01

Customer data was scattered

Customer records lived across different Quick products, with no single place to manage them.

02

Customer interactions lacked context

Businesses couldn't get a complete view of their interactions and relationship with a customer over time.

03

Customer data wasn't actionable

Businesses couldn't easily turn their existing contacts into targeted groups for specific business activities.

04

Managing customers was inefficient

Finding, organizing, and working with the right customers became increasingly manual as the customer base grew.

04 · Goal

Goal

Create one connected customer system where businesses can manage, understand, organize, and activate their customer data across Quick Hub.

01

Centralize

Bring customer records from across Quick Hub into one shared system. Businesses should no longer need to manage the same customers separately across different products.

02

Understand

Give businesses a clearer view of each customer beyond basic contact details. Customer context and interactions should be easier to understand from one place.

03

Organize

Make it easier to search, filter, and manage customers as the database grows. Businesses should be able to quickly find the customers they need to work with.

04

Activate

Make customer data useful beyond the CRM itself. Businesses should be able to use their customers across Quick Hub, particularly for Campaigns and Broadcasts.

05 · Approach

Approach

Understanding the problem from multiple perspectives

Before designing the CRM, I looked at the problem from both product and business perspectives. I used competitive analysis and insights from business owners, the sales team, and PMs to understand what the CRM needed to solve.

01

Competitive analysis

I studied established CRMs to understand patterns for customer profiles, activity, filtering, tagging, and segmentation. This helped me identify useful patterns without simply copying another product.

02

Business owner insights

I spoke with business owners to understand how they manage customers and what information they actually need. This highlighted the need to organize customers, understand interactions, and find the right customers quickly.

03

Sales team insights

I worked with the sales team to understand how customer information is used in everyday workflows. Their input helped identify the customer context and information needed for follow-ups and customer management.

04

PM & product

I worked with PMs to understand the existing product structure and how CRM would connect with Quick Chat, Quick Campaign, and Quick Ads. This helped define CRM as a shared customer system across Quick Hub.

What this helped me define
01 · Shared customer record

One central place for customer information.

02 · Customer context

Go beyond basic contact details and understand interactions.

03 · Reusable customer data

Make customer data usable across Quick Hub, especially Campaigns and Broadcasts.

06 · Design

Turning the product decision into the CRM experience

6.1

Start with an overview

The dashboard gives business owners a quick view of their customer base and what needs attention, before they move into any individual record.

Total contacts Total tags Today's follow-up Upcoming follow-up Source distribution
6.2

Turn Contacts into a workspace

A basic contact table answers "Can I find this customer?" A CRM should also help users organize groups of customers and take action on them.

6.3

Make the customer the primary record

01

Who is this customer?

02

What do we know about them?

03

What has happened with them?

04

What can we do next?

The profile answers those four in a fixed order. Identity and the available actions sit at the top of the left panel, the information the business holds and its tags below them, and everything time-based occupies the main area — so nothing important sits behind navigation.

The customer became the primary unit of the CRM, rather than the contact row.

Alternative I rejected Separate channel-specific views

Splitting the record by channel organizes information tidily, but it turns one relationship into several partial ones.

6.4

Give the customer a history, not just a profile

The profile shows Who the customer is
The activity history shows The relationship with that customer
WhatsApp interactions Broadcasts Campaigns Replies Calls Notes Follow-ups Tags Customer updates

All of it lands in one chronological view, but not at the same weight. Communication needs enough context to recognize a conversation; record changes stay compact. Making every event visually equal would have produced a log rather than a relationship.

Communication

Broadcast and campaign entries carry the most context: the campaign, the template used and enough of the message to recognize it.

Follow-up · Note

Enough detail to understand what was planned or written, and when, without competing with the messages around it.

Tags · Updates

Compact single lines. Record changes belong in the history, but they are not what the reader is scanning for.

6.5

Connect customer context to action

Customer context is useful when it helps the business decide what to do next. I structured each activity around the type of interaction, while keeping follow-ups connected to the same customer context.

Call

Understand conversations, outcomes, and recordings.

Email

See campaigns, scheduled emails, received emails, and individual messages.

Notes

Capture internal customer context that may influence the next action.

1 / 3

From context to action

Customer interactions provide the context needed to decide what happens next. Follow-ups turn that context into scheduled, assigned work without separating the action from the customer record.

07 · Data Migration

Making customer data ready for CRM

How do we bring existing customer data into a structured CRM?

A centralized CRM still needs existing customers

Businesses already had customer information before Quick CRM existed.

Moving to a centralized CRM meant giving them a practical way to bring that existing data with them.

Bring existing customer data into CRM

Businesses can bring their existing customer list into CRM using a CSV file. The import begins in Contacts, where the customer base already lives.

Because spreadsheets don't share one structure, each CSV column has to be connected to the right CRM field, and the data has to be checked before any customer records are created.

01
Upload

Select the spreadsheet. The import starts from Contacts, where the customer base already lives.

02
Map

Map spreadsheet columns to CRM fields, with a default mapping proposed and the required fields marked.

03
Review

Identify incorrect or duplicate records before importing. They are named, and offered as a CSV to fix outside the product.

04
Import

Create the customer records only after review, with the progress state showing how many are being added.

1 / 4

The result is a safer path from an existing customer spreadsheet to a centralized CRM database.

08 · A New Problem

Finding customers wasn't enough

Once customer data was centralized, businesses could search and filter their customers. But they could find a group without being able to keep that group for later.

A filter answered a question right now. It didn't create a reusable audience for later.

09 · Segmentation

Having all customers in one place wasn't enough

Once customer data was centralized, businesses could manage their customers from one place.

But when running a campaign or broadcast, they often needed to include or exclude a specific group of customers.

For example Send this campaign to customers who came from WhatsApp, but exclude customers already tagged as VIP.

The existing way of building this audience involved repeatedly filtering and working through customer lists, making the process lengthy and difficult to reuse.

Finding a simpler way to define audiences

I looked at how CRM and marketing products handle audience selection and segmentation and compared those patterns with the workflow businesses were trying to accomplish in Quick Hub.

The existing filtering approach could help find customers, but it was not an efficient way to repeatedly build and manage the same audience.

I introduced Segments to turn audience selection into a reusable system.

From filtering customers to defining an audience

Filter Find customers
—Narrows the customer list for the current task —Useful when you need to find customers right now —The criteria are applied to the current view —You need to repeat the filtering when you need the same audience again
Segment Define an audience
—Stores the rules that define the audience —Can be reused across Quick Hub workflows —Does not require manually maintaining a separate customer list —Membership changes as customer data changes
Segment · VIP Customers Tag = VIP
Today Customer A Customer B Customer C
Later Customer A Customer B Customer C Customer D receives the VIP tag

Customer D automatically becomes part of the Segment. If a customer no longer matches the rule, they can automatically leave it.

The business defines the rules. CRM keeps the audience updated.

Building the audience

With Segments, businesses can define an audience using information already stored in CRM. The process starts with an empty audience, and as conditions are added the matching customer count shows how large that audience is before it is saved.

01
Choose the first rule

Pick the customer information that defines membership.

02
Narrow the audience

Add a second condition to reach a smaller group.

03
Combine conditions

A second group gives a different set of conditions a way into the same audience.

04
Verify the audience

Check the customers the rules currently match before saving.

10 · Outcome

From scattered contact records to a reusable customer system

Before
  • Separate contact tables in Quick Chat, Campaign and Ads
  • Basic contact information
  • Duplicate records
  • Export and import between products
  • No shared customer context
  • Limited organization
After
  • Centralized customer records
  • Reusable across Quick Hub
  • Customer profile and context
  • Interaction history
  • Follow-ups
  • Tags and filtering
  • Dynamic segmentation
  • Reusable audiences in Campaigns and Broadcasts

Quick CRM gave businesses one place to manage customers, understand their relationship with the business, and turn customer data into reusable audiences across Quick Hub.

11 · My Role

What I owned

Product thinking

Defined the role of CRM within the Quick Hub ecosystem and considered cross-product customer data.

Information architecture

Structured the customer experience around information, context, activity and action.

UX

Worked across contact management, customer profiles, history, follow-ups, bulk import, filtering and segmentation.

UI

Designed interface hierarchy, interactions, states, components and detailed product UI.

Prototyping

Used prototypes to explore and communicate the product experience.

12 · Reflection

The problem changed as the product became more useful

01
Fragmented contacts Centralized customer system
02
Customer records lacked relationship context Customer history, follow-up and action
03
Finding customers wasn't enough Dynamic segmentation and reusable audiences

The important question wasn't only what information should appear on the screen. It was what information needed to become reusable system behavior.

That changed how I approached CRM design: not as a collection of screens, but as a system that connects customer information, relationships and actions across the product.
